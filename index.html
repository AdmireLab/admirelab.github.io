ADMIRE Research Group

ADMIRE (Adaptive Distributed Multi-agent Intelligence via Recursive Evolution) is a research group at the State Key Laboratory of Complex and Critical Software Environment at Beihang University. The group is led by Prof. Wenjun Wu.

We study adaptive multi-agent systems in complex environments, focusing on how agents remember, learn, reason, collaborate and evolve. Our research covers software infrastructure for agent coordination and controlled recursive self-improvement, embodied swarm intelligence for drones and robots, and scientific discovery through collaboration among theory, computation and experiment agents.

About the Lab

ADMIRE is based at Beihang University’s State Key Laboratory of Complex and Critical Software Environment.

Research Areas

Built on the RSI foundation and governance of the software space, the group connects the physical/embodied and scientific-discovery spaces, developing key technologies and system frameworks for adaptive agent collectives in complex environments.

Software Foundation and governance for agent collectives

Coordinate agents through Agentic Mesh and support controlled recursive self-improvement.

Research details

A unified runtime and governance foundation (Agentic Mesh) for agents and services across the software, embodied and scientific-discovery spaces, with controlled recursive self-improvement (RSI) through isolated experiments, independent evaluation and progressive release.

  • Agentic Mesh governance: runtime, lifecycle, gateway, telemetry, memory and orchestration for agent and service collectives.
  • Controlled self-improvement: candidates are generated and tested in isolated sandboxes, filtered by constraints, independently re-evaluated, then released progressively with rollback.
  • Domain agents: service composition, microservice root-cause analysis and AIOps, evolving as typical applications on the foundation.
Agentic Mesh RSI foundation RCA Agents

Physical Systems Embodied collective intelligence

Study coordinated control, path planning and validation on physical drones and robots.

Research details

For UAVs, robots and unmanned systems, we combine high-level LLM cognition with multi-agent reinforcement learning, physical priors, safety constraints and real-world feedback.

  • Stable control, team consensus, path planning and spatial reasoning enable deployable multi-robot collaboration.
  • Symmetry, potential fields and Lyapunov stability improve training efficiency and safety.
  • LLM agents generate rewards, organize training, package models and support sim-to-real transfer.
UAV Swarm MARL Sim-to-Real

Scientific Discovery Agentic Scientist

Bring theory, computing and experiment agents together to support research automation.

Research details

Scientific problems become verifiable agent workflows, connecting theory, computation, experiments and feedback for scientific computing, AI4Sci and laboratory automation.

  • Theory, computing and experiment agents collaborate on literature, hypotheses, simulations and experimental design.
  • For PDE solvers and fluid simulation, we connect retrieval, planning, code generation, execution and correction.
  • ScienceOS, intelligent computing and robotic/multimodal laboratory equipment support reproducible research automation.

04Computing & Simulation

Simulation & Robot Software

Existing capabilities

The Tianji platform supports simulations with hundreds of agents using Unreal scenes and containerized services. Existing experiments cover MARL pursuit, robot navigation, manipulation demonstrations, policy training and real-robot testing.

In development

Harness is being developed to unify high-level tasks, commands and status across heterogeneous platforms through chassis and quadruped SDK adapters. The initial target is collaborative language-guided navigation with one vehicle and one quadruped, followed by UAV integration.

The Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab pipeline is under development, spanning modeling, unified observations, collaborative policy training, robustness validation, ROS 2 deployment and evaluation feedback, while reusing established low-level controllers.
